Reflection

Reflection

The experience, the challenges, what I'm taking with me, and what I'd tell anyone starting something similar.

Experience, Challenges, and Highlights

Watching a fully remote team run day to day and keep growing was one of the most rewarding parts of this internship. Remote work has its own learning curve: communication takes more intention when you can't just walk over and ask. Engineering was the hardest department to wrap my head around, especially MAPS, the server running Argos, IntelliCheck, and FormFusion behind the scenes.

MAPS handles licensing, security, and every database connection for the whole product suite. Seeing how much infrastructure sits behind something as simple as opening an app was eye-opening. Data Engineering had similar complexity, pulling data from dozens of disconnected campus systems into something usable. Finance coordinated budget input across departments where one missing detail throws off the whole picture. Even Support, which seemed simple at first, took real judgment, triaging issues almost like an emergency room.

The clear highlight was the Argos workshop week with Professional Services: learning SQL and finally seeing how the product I'd heard about all summer actually works. Every department left me with something worth keeping.

The SEA mock product tied it all together: every rotation added to the same dashboard concept, so what I learned applied to one continuous project instead of staying abstract. The Friday bonus courses (Philanthropy, Communications, Connections, and Business) ran alongside every department week and added relationship-building and communication skills that mattered just as much as the technical ones.

Insights and Advice Gained from Employees

That relationship-building thread from the bonus courses carried into the best advice I got from actual Evisions employees this summer. The biggest lesson: real connections matter. They open doors, and they make the work more enjoyable. Evisions has a strong sense of community, and people willing to share their time made me more confident and motivated throughout.

Employees also passed along solid career advice: tailor your resume to the actual job instead of using one generic version, quantify your achievements with real numbers, and walk into interviews with a few strong stories ready instead of an answer for everything. One thing that stuck: researching who's interviewing you and finding real common ground turns an interview into a conversation people remember.

Next Steps After the Internship

With that advice in mind, here's where things are headed next. I'm applying to jobs with an open mind rather than locking onto one exact role or industry too early. I plan to build on the connections made here, keep developing the skills I picked up around data, reporting, and how a product moves from idea to launch, and keep working on passion projects I can point to and show. I'm aiming for a long-term career with room to grow rather than optimizing for whichever offer comes first, while staying curious about where that direction leads. If you’re hiring or just want to connect, my resume and contact info are one click away.

Advice for Anyone in a Similar Position

If someone were about to start something similar, here's what I'd actually tell them.

  • Take remote communication seriously: over-communicate and ask questions early instead of waiting for context to come to you.
  • Don't shy away from departments that feel too technical at first. Sitting with the discomfort is usually how it starts to click.
  • Treat every conversation as a chance to build a real connection instead of just collecting information.
  • Start applying the career advice early: tailored resumes, real stories, interviews as conversations.
  • Stay curious and let yourself enjoy it. The people matter as much as the technical skills.
